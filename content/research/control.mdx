Perception and control meet in the presence of delay. Once you allow
that sensing, compute, and network communication each introduce
non-negligible lag, the classical separation between "figure out what
the world is" and "decide what to do about it" becomes untenable —
the two problems interact through the delay.

This track collects the stability analyses and predicate-binding gates
that remain after audit. No verified result currently links their
closed-loop stability to the topology of the upstream representation.

## The ORTSF framework

**ORTSF** (Ontological Real-Time Semantic Fabric) is a family of
predicate-binding operators whose job is to synthesise a control
signal from an ONN latent state while preserving the meaning encoded in
that state. The operators carry explicit delay budgets.

<Callout tone="warning" title="What the certificate actually is">
Earlier drafts stated ORTSF stability "in the language of the learned
ontology's cohomology", and claimed the ONN total loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}$ *is* a constructive Lyapunov function
resolving the Massera–Kurzweil problem. **That coupling did not survive
audit.** The implemented proof is much narrower: one additive delayed
scalar recurrence in an $H^\perp$ mode, with a sufficient Schur-stability
condition. It is **decoupled** from the ontology's cohomology and is not
yet a coupled, system-level certificate. See the
[delay-margin certificate](/onn/theorem-delay-robust-bound/) and the
[cohomological-stability audit](/onn/theorem-cohomological-stability/).
</Callout>

## What has been shown

- **A scoped sufficient condition.** For the scalar recurrence
  $e[t+1] = \rho e[t] - K_c e[t-d]$, the condition
  $\rho + |K_c| < 1$ is sufficient for Schur stability for every integer
  delay $d \geq 1$. This does not establish coupled multimode or
  deployed-controller stability; the $B$-operator bound, mapping $K_c$
  to physical PD gains, and full-system lift remain open.
- **What was withdrawn.** The "constructive resolution of the
  Massera–Kurzweil problem", the specific `τ_max = 177 μs` / 3M-node
  bound, and the cohomological-Lyapunov reading are **not reproducible
  from the current research source** and are retired — see the
  [ONN research status](/onn/onn-research-status-2026/).

## The budget-first view

Before reaching for a full stability analysis it pays to decompose
the end-to-end delay of a perception-control loop into a *budget*: a
small set of line items each tied to a term in the analysis and each
independently measurable. A first pass:

- **Sensing delay** $\tau_s$ — from physical event to ready observation.
- **Perception delay** $\tau_p$ — from observation to latent state update.
- **Decision delay** $\tau_d$ — from latent state to control signal.
- **Actuation delay** $\tau_a$ — from signal to effect on plant.

With this structure the stability margin can be stated per-term,
which is more actionable than a scalar bound.
