**Unified Latent Representation (ULR)** is the current main research
programme. It asks four connected questions:

1. **Sharing** — what structure is actually shared across models, modalities,
   tasks, and seeds?
2. **Identity** — when are two representations the same after the correct
   gauge, normalisation, and observable have been declared?
3. **Role** — when can an observer distinguish learning from inference through
   permitted interventions and output ports?
4. **Formation** — when does a persistent, transportable organisation emerge
   rather than a metric merely changing?

The current canonical conclusion is deliberately restrictive. In the tested
registry, none of seven candidate carriers leaves a neural-specific residual
after the strongest typed baseline is applied. Canon 24 therefore records the
ontology verdict **NO** and adopts typed pluralism as the working position.
This is not a metaphysical claim that organisation can never exist. It is a
claim about what the current evidence licenses.

The programme remains active because the negative verdict sharpens the next
question: which laws constrain unobserved typed responses, and do those laws
survive predictive-state, causal, dynamical, symmetry, and routing baselines?
