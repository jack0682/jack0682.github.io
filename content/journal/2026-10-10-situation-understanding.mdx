## The starting question

How do we decide what matters in a situation before we fully understand it?
Explaining relevance through a goal or past experience leaves another
question: where did that goal, or the criterion for using that experience,
come from?

This inquiry begins before a particular robot implementation. Its current
question is: **when does something happening differently to a system become
something going well or badly for that system?**

## Three distinctions to keep open

- **Selection:** a system responds differently to different conditions.
- **Functional relevance:** a difference affects the conditions under which
  its activity can continue.
- **Evaluation:** the system adjusts its relations in response to those effects.

This is a provisional way to organize the question, not a demonstrated
sequence in the origin of human judgment. Differential response alone does
not establish importance. Continued existence alone does not establish that
the system treats its own continuation as valuable.

## The objection that matters

An observer can describe a change as useful or harmful without the system
itself making any evaluation. Learned attention and reaction may also explain
behavior that appears to involve a separate relevance judgment; a verbal
explanation may be constructed afterward. Behavioral success does not by
itself choose between these explanations.

The next question is whether the possibility of error is enough to establish
a norm, or whether a system must distinguish and correct that error. The
inquiry remains **conceptual and unresolved**. No empirical account of human
cognition, subjective experience, or robot understanding is claimed here.

For the related question of what learning forms and what observations can
identify, see [the learned-structure research update](/journal/2026-10-11-learned-structure/).
